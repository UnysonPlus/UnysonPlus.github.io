# Roadmap

Twenty-four phases, built in order on one scene file, so nothing made early has to be redone later. The Studio is in
beta at [kinetic-scenes.unysonplus.com](https://kinetic-scenes.unysonplus.com/), and all but a handful of tasks are built:
two cloud options waiting until people ask for them, two ways to learn from a video link, and a benchmark against
hand-made videos that is still running. The [guide pages](./index.mdx) describe how to use each feature.

## Order of work

1. **Foundations.** The scene file, the player and the responsive stage, because everything else reads or writes them.
2. **Editor.** The canvas, the timeline, the inspector and the presets: enough to build a real hero by hand.
3. **Export.** Standalone HTML and an embed snippet, each recording the player version it was made with.
4. **Unyson+ integration.** The Kinetic Scene element with an **Open in Kinetic Scenes** round trip.
5. **AI.** Prompts produce scene files through the [AI Dev Kit](/ai-dev-kit), so every result can be edited
   on the timeline.
6. **3D and finer control.** three.js layers, a keyframe and curve editor, and scroll-linked playback.
7. **Later.** Video export, click-to-advance presentations and share links.
8. **Sound and voice.** Built-in sound effects, voice-overs with their words, styles, and scenes made from a voice-over.
9. **Fluid motion.** Transitions, the camera, paths, beat sync, captions and the motion that hand-made reels use.
10. **Product tours and interactive scenes.** Links, hover effects, the looping ending, pinning to the stage, cursors,
    focus and screenshots that come alive.
11. **The AI director.** The AI plans with you, builds, checks, looks at rendered frames and fixes what it sees, with
    tools from the kit and only the subscription you already have; plus a quick scene from any website.
12. **Video mode.** Formats, transparent overlays for video editors, and videos that feel like one camera move.
13. **Video editing effects.** The effects short-form editors use on talking-head videos, as presets and pieces.
14. **Faster, and video without the kit.** Speed-ups and video export in the browser.
15. **Approachable, and proven.** Easy for someone who has never used a timeline, and measured against real work.
16. **Reels from your footage.** A long, unedited video turned into a finished short reel.
17. **Direct by pointing.** Point at the frame to change it, and choose a direction before the AI builds.
18. **More to animate.** Vector animations from the free libraries and 3D objects made from a description.
19. **Motion styles, and a stricter review.** Recognised motion-design styles by name, and a tougher check of the result.
20. **Real screen demos.** Screen recordings framed automatically, with a voice-over and a written guide from one take.
21. **Projects you share with your AI agent.** Projects as folders on your computer that the Studio and an agent share.
22. **Agents that edit like a careful person.** Notes, timing at a glance and checks of every voice line.
23. **Learn from great reels.** The rhythm of a reel you like, reels from a few beats, and how a reel was built.
24. **A jumpstart for every video.** One start screen for every kind of video, plus painted, paper, physics, long videos
    and talking-head effects.

The plan and the reasons behind it are on the [overview](./index.mdx); what already exists elsewhere is on the
[research](./research.md) page.
